Module 2: Fundamental Concepts of the ISMS

1. What Is an ISMS?

An Information Security Management System (ISMS) is a structured approach to protecting an organisation's information through policies, processes, people, technology, and risk management.

An effective ISMS is risk-based, supported by top management, and continually improved.

2. The PDCA Cycle

The Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle supports the operation and continual improvement of an ISMS.

Plan: Establish the ISMS, define its scope, assess risks, and plan treatments.

Do: Implement security controls, procedures, and awareness activities.

Check: Monitor performance, evaluate results, and conduct internal audits.

Act: Address nonconformities and improve the ISMS.


The PDCA cycle helps organisations identify weaknesses and improve their information security management over time.

3. ISO/IEC 27001 vs. ISO/IEC 27002

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 specifies the requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an ISMS. It is the basis for certification.

ISO/IEC 27002:2022 provides guidance on information security controls and supports their implementation.


4. Key Learning Points

An ISMS combines people, processes, policies, and technology.

Information security should be managed according to identified risks.

Top management commitment and continual improvement are essential.

Internal audits are part of the Check phase and relate to Clause 9.

Corrective action and improvement relate to Clause 10.
