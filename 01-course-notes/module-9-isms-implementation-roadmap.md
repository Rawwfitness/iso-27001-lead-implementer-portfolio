# Module 9: The ISMS Implementation Roadmap
 
## 1. Introduction
 
Implementing an Information Security Management System (ISMS) requires a structured approach. The organisation must establish its objectives, assess information security risks, implement appropriate controls, evaluate performance, and prepare for an external certification audit.
 
The course presents a five-phase implementation roadmap.
 
## 2. Phase 1: Initiation and Planning
 
**Suggested timeline: Months 1–2**
 
The first phase establishes the foundation for the ISMS.
 
Key activities include:
 
 
- **Obtain management commitment:** Explain the business benefits of ISO/IEC 27001 and secure the necessary support and resources.
 
- **Define the ISMS scope — Clause 4.3:** Identify the systems, departments, locations, and activities covered by the ISMS.
 
- **Develop the Information Security Policy — Clause 5.2:** Prepare a policy that establishes the organisation's overall direction for information security.
 
- **Form the implementation team:** Involve relevant functions such as IT, Human Resources, Legal, Facilities, and Operations.
 

 
## 3. Phase 2: Risk Assessment and Treatment
 
**Suggested timeline: Months 3–4**
 
This phase identifies information security risks and determines how they will be addressed.
 
Key activities include:
 
 
- **Conduct risk assessment — Clause 6.1.2:** Identify and evaluate risks affecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information.
 
- **Develop the Risk Treatment Plan — Clause 6.1.3:** Select appropriate ways to address risks that require treatment.
 
- **Prepare the Statement of Applicability (SoA):** Document decisions about Annex A controls, including their applicability, justifications, and implementation status.
 

 
The organisation should base its control decisions on its risk assessment, treatment decisions, and applicable requirements.
 
## 4. Phase 3: Implementation of Controls
 
**Suggested timeline: Months 5–7**
 
During this phase, the organisation puts its plans into practice.
 
Key activities include:
 
 
- Implementing selected organisational, people, physical, and technological controls.
 
- Developing procedures and work instructions for security activities.
 
- Providing staff training and awareness activities.
 
- Assigning responsibilities for operating and maintaining controls.
 

 
Examples of implementation activities include introducing access badges, deploying data loss prevention tools, and managing mobile devices securely.
 
## 5. Phase 4: Monitoring and Internal Audit
 
**Suggested timeline: Month 8**
 
Before an external certification audit, the organisation evaluates its ISMS and addresses identified weaknesses.
 
Key activities include:
 
 
- **Monitor performance — Clause 9.1:** Collect and evaluate information about ISMS performance.
 
- **Conduct internal audits — Clause 9.2:** Assess whether the ISMS meets applicable requirements and is effectively implemented and maintained.
 
- **Address nonconformities — Clause 10.2:** Investigate findings and implement appropriate corrective actions.
 
- **Conduct a management review — Clause 9.3:** Present relevant ISMS performance information to top management and record review results and decisions.
 

 
Internal audits must be conducted objectively and impartially.
 
## 6. Phase 5: Certification Audit
 
**Suggested timeline: Month 9**
 
Once the organisation has implemented its ISMS and assessed its readiness, it can engage an appropriate certification body for an external certification audit.
 
### 6.1 Stage 1 Audit — Documentation and Readiness
 
The Stage 1 audit primarily evaluates the ISMS documentation and readiness for the Stage 2 audit.
 
Documentation may include:
 
 
- ISMS scope.
 
- Information Security Policy.
 
- Risk assessment methodology and results.
 
- Risk Treatment Plan.
 
- Statement of Applicability.
 
- Internal audit programme and relevant results.
 
- Management review records.
 

 
### 6.2 Stage 2 Audit — Implementation and Effectiveness
 
The Stage 2 audit evaluates whether the ISMS has been implemented and is operating effectively.
 
The auditor may review records, interview personnel, observe processes, and examine evidence to determine whether the organisation conforms to applicable requirements.
 
### 6.3 Certification Decision
 
The certification body makes the certification decision based on the audit results and its applicable certification process.
 
Certification is not automatic after completing the two audit stages. Any nonconformities must be handled in accordance with the certification body's requirements before certification can be granted.
 
## 7. Key Learning Points
 
 
- ISMS implementation benefits from a structured roadmap.
 
- Management commitment and a clearly defined scope establish the foundation.
 
- Risk assessment and treatment guide decisions about information security controls.
 
- Selected controls must be implemented and supported by appropriate procedures and training.
 
- Monitoring, internal audits, corrective actions, and management reviews help assess readiness.
 
- Stage 1 focuses primarily on documentation and readiness.
 
- Stage 2 evaluates the implementation and effectiveness of the ISMS.
 
- The certification body is responsible for making the certification decision.
 

 
## Conclusion
 
A structured implementation roadmap helps an organisation coordinate people, processes, resources, and security controls. By progressing through planning, risk treatment, implementation, performance evaluation, and external auditing, an organisation can prepare its ISMS for formal certification assessment.
