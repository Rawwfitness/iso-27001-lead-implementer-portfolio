# Module 7: Performance Evaluation — Clause 9
 
## 1. Introduction
 
Performance evaluation helps an organisation determine whether its Information Security Management System (ISMS) is effective and achieving its information security objectives.
 
Clause 9 covers three main areas:
 
 
- Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation.
 
- Internal audits.
 
- Management reviews.
 

 
## 2. Monitoring and Measurement — Clause 9.1
 
The organisation must evaluate information security performance and the effectiveness of its ISMS.
 
To do this, it must determine:
 
 
- **What** needs to be monitored and measured.
 
- **How** monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation will be performed.
 
- **When** monitoring and measurement will take place.
 
- **Who** will perform monitoring and measurement.
 
- **When** the results will be analysed and evaluated.
 
- **Who** will analyse and evaluate the results.
 

 
### Practical Examples
 
An organisation might monitor:
 
 
- The number of information security incidents.
 
- The percentage of employees who complete security awareness training.
 
- System availability and uptime.
 
- Progress towards information security objectives.
 

 
Measurements should help the organisation understand whether its security objectives are being achieved. Collecting data that does not provide meaningful information about security performance may offer little value.
 
## 3. Internal Audit — Clause 9.2
 
An internal audit helps an organisation determine whether its ISMS meets applicable requirements and is effectively implemented and maintained.
 
### Internal Audit Programme
 
The organisation must conduct internal audits at planned intervals and establish an audit programme covering relevant arrangements, including:
 
 
- Audit frequency and methods.
 
- Responsibilities and planning requirements.
 
- Audit scope and criteria.
 
- Reporting of results.
 
- Retention of documented information as evidence of the audit programme and results.
 

 
### Objectivity and Impartiality
 
Internal auditors must conduct audits objectively and impartially.
 
For example, if a Lead Implementer developed the ISMS and implemented its controls, that person should not audit their own work. The organisation should arrange for an appropriately independent auditor, such as a qualified person from another department or an external consultant.
 
Internal audits are different from external certification audits, which are conducted by an external certification body as part of the certification process.
 
## 4. Management Review — Clause 9.3
 
Top management must review the ISMS at planned intervals to ensure its continuing suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness.
 
The Lead Implementer may help prepare the review agenda, gather performance information, and present relevant findings to management.
 
### 4.1 Required Inputs
 
Management review considers information such as:
 
 
- The status of actions from previous management reviews.
 
- Changes in internal and external issues.
 
- Changes in the needs and expectations of interested parties.
 
- Information security performance and trends, including nonconformities and corrective actions.
 
- Monitoring and measurement results.
 
- Internal audit results.
 
- Progress towards information security objectives.
 
- Feedback from interested parties.
 
- Risk assessment results and the status of the risk treatment plan.
 
- Opportunities for continual improvement.
 

 
### 4.2 Required Outputs
 
The management review must produce decisions and actions relating to:
 
 
- Opportunities for continual improvement.
 
- Any necessary changes to the ISMS.
 

 
These may include decisions about resource allocation or budget where needed.
 
Documented information providing evidence of the management review results must be retained.
 
## 5. Key Learning Points
 
 
- Clause 9.1 focuses on evaluating information security performance and ISMS effectiveness.
 
- Clause 9.2 requires internal audits at planned intervals.
 
- Internal auditors must maintain objectivity and impartiality.
 
- Clause 9.3 requires top management to review the ISMS at planned intervals.
 
- Management review considers audit results, security performance, objectives, risks, and improvement opportunities.
 
- Documented evidence of audits and management reviews must be retained.
 

 
## Conclusion
 
Performance evaluation helps an organisation identify weaknesses, assess progress towards security objectives, and make informed decisions about improving its ISMS. Monitoring, independent internal audits, and management reviews all contribute to continual improvement.
