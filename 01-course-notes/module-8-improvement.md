Module 8: Improvement — Clause 10

1. Introduction

Clause 10 focuses on improving the suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness of an organisation's Information Security Management System (ISMS).

It covers two main areas:

- Clause 10.1: Continual improvement.
- Clause 10.2: Nonconformity and corrective action.

2. Continual Improvement — Clause 10.1

Continual improvement means regularly identifying opportunities to make the ISMS more effective and suitable for the organisation's needs.

Improvement is not limited to correcting mistakes. It also involves proactively strengthening information security.

Examples of Continual Improvement

- Automating manual user access reviews.
- Updating outdated cryptographic methods.
- Extending the ISMS scope when the organisation's activities change.
- Improving security awareness training using current threat information.
- Applying lessons learned from incidents and previous assessments.

The objective is to ensure that the ISMS continues to meet the organisation's information security needs as risks and circumstances change.

3. Nonconformity and Corrective Action — Clause 10.2

3.1 What Is a Nonconformity?

A nonconformity is the failure to fulfil a requirement.

It may involve a requirement of ISO/IEC 27001, an internal security policy, or another applicable requirement.

Examples

- Employees sharing passwords in violation of the organisation's access control policy.
- Failing to conduct an internal audit as planned.
- Leaving a server unpatched in violation of established vulnerability management requirements.

3.2 The Corrective Action Process

When a nonconformity occurs, the organisation must respond systematically.

Step 1: React to the Nonconformity

Take action to control and correct the issue and deal with its consequences.

Example: Isolate a vulnerable server to reduce immediate security exposure.

Step 2: Identify the Root Cause

Evaluate whether action is needed to eliminate the cause of the nonconformity and prevent recurrence or occurrence elsewhere.

Example: Investigate why the server was not patched. Possible causes could include unclear responsibilities, failed patch management tools, or inadequate monitoring.

Step 3: Implement Corrective Action

Take the necessary action to address the identified cause.

Example: Assign clear patch management responsibilities and introduce automated alerts.

Step 4: Review Effectiveness

Check whether the corrective action has worked and whether the problem has been prevented from recurring.

Example: Review patch management records after implementation to confirm that updates are being applied as required.

Step 5: Update the ISMS Where Necessary

Make changes to the ISMS if required.

Example: Revise the vulnerability management procedure to address the identified weakness.

The organisation must retain documented information about the nonconformity, the actions taken, and the results of corrective action.

4. Practical Example: Accidental Disclosure of Customer Data

Imagine an employee accidentally emails a spreadsheet containing customers' personally identifiable information (PII) to the wrong external recipient.

The organisation recalls the email and takes steps to limit the exposure.

However, resolving the immediate incident is not necessarily enough.

The organisation should then:

1. Investigate how the information was sent to the wrong recipient.
2. Determine the underlying cause.
3. Decide what corrective action is necessary.
4. Implement the action.
5. Review whether it has been effective.
6. Update relevant procedures or other parts of the ISMS where necessary.

Possible improvements could include clearer recipient verification procedures, staff training, or appropriate data loss prevention measures.

5. Key Learning Points

- Clause 10.1 covers continual improvement.
- Clause 10.2 covers nonconformity and corrective action.
- A nonconformity is the failure to fulfil a requirement.
- Correcting an immediate problem does not necessarily eliminate its root cause.
- Corrective action aims to prevent recurrence or occurrence elsewhere.
- The effectiveness of corrective actions must be reviewed.
- Relevant documented information must be retained.

Conclusion

An effective ISMS requires organisations to learn from security failures and continually improve their processes. By addressing root causes, checking corrective action effectiveness, and making necessary changes, organisations can strengthen their information security management over time.
